The idea

One question, taken seriously.

Effective altruism is a research field and a practical community. It aims to find the best ways to help others, and to put them into practice.

The question

With limited time and money, how can we help others the most?

Most of us want to make things better. Fewer of us stop to check whether what we are doing works, or whether something else would work better.

Effective altruism is the habit of checking. The answers draw on evidence and careful reasoning, and they stay open to challenge.

Changing your mind is allowed. Encouraged, even.

Where the ideas lead

Causes we talk about

Effective altruism is a way of asking the question, not a fixed list of answers. These are three areas people in the community often work on.

Global health and development

People use evidence and careful comparisons to explore ways of improving health and reducing poverty.

Animal welfare

People consider how to reduce animal suffering and improve the treatment of animals.

AI and other global catastrophic risks

People study ways to reduce risks from powerful technologies and other threats that could affect many people.

Your career, in dots

80,000

hours in a typical career. Each dot is 1,000 of them. That is roughly 40 years, 50 weeks a year, 40 hours a week.

Where they go is one of the biggest decisions you will make, and uni is when most people make it.

The figure comes from 80,000 Hours, a careers nonprofit named after it.

Fair questions

Things people ask us

Do I have to agree with all of it?

No. People in effective altruism disagree with each other constantly, and we like it that way. If you think the whole idea has a flaw, come and tell us. That is exactly the conversation we want.

Is this just about donating money?

No. Giving is one way to help. So are your career, your research and your time. We talk about all of them.

Do I need to know philosophy or economics?

No background needed. Our sessions are built so that a first-time visitor can take part straight away.

Isn't effective altruism controversial?

It has critics, and some of the criticism is fair. We would rather talk about it openly than pretend it does not exist. Bring your hardest questions.
