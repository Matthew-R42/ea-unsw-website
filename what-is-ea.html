EA asks one simple question:

With limited time and money, how can we help others the most?

Effective altruism is about finding the best ways to help others
(and then actually doing them).

Part research

Which problems are biggest? What actually works? What is everyone else ignoring?

Part action

People pointing their careers, their donations and their spare time at those problems.

Say you want to help people who are blind.

1

guide dog, fully trained

or

1,000

operations that give someone their sight back

Both cost about $50,000.
One helps a thousand times more people.

Noticing gaps like that, and acting on them, is the whole idea. Try your own numbers →

The surgery side has a UNSW link: Fred Hollows, whose foundation does these operations, taught eye medicine at UNSW from 1965.

$50 per operation, double the lowest figure from the Fred Hollows Foundation, to be conservative. $50,000 per guide dog, from Guide Dogs Australia.

Four ideas hold it together

  1. Numbers matter.Helping a thousand people beats helping one.Scope sensitivity
  2. Everyone counts.A stranger on the other side of the world matters as much as one down the road.Impartiality
  3. Want the truth more than the win.Go looking for where you might be wrong, and change your mind when you find it.Scout mindset
  4. You can't do everything.An hour or a dollar spent here can't be spent there.Trade-offs

The same four that effectivealtruism.org lists as what unites the community.

So which problem? Ask three questions.

Is it big?

How many does it touch, and how badly?

Is it overlooked?

If a crowd is already on it, one more person adds less.

Is it solvable?

Is there something that would actually work?

A problem that scores on all three is where your help goes furthest. Mix and match them, from malaria to Mario 64 speedruns →

Things people ask us

Do I have to agree with all of it?

No. People in effective altruism disagree all the time, and we like it that way.

Is this just about donating money?

No. Your career, your research and your time count as much as your money.

Do I need to know philosophy or economics?

No. Every session is built so a first-timer can join in straight away.

Isn't effective altruism controversial?

It has critics, and some of them have a point. Week 5 of our fellowship is all about it.
